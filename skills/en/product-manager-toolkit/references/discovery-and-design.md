# Discovery and Design Resources

Use for the `discovery` route. Access snapshot: 2026-09-17. These cards distinguish practical web applications from readable professional methods. Paid offers linked from a page are outside scope.

## GOV.UK Service Manual — research planning and interviews

- **Open:** [Plan user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service) and [in-depth interviews](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews).
- **Free scope:** public, readable guidance; no course, account, or template download is required.
- **Professional grounding:** operational guidance for government service teams; the [team roles specification](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/the-team/service-manager.html) explicitly includes product managers and user researchers.
- **Use:** turn assumptions into research questions; choose relevant participant groups; prepare prompts about a recent real event, follow-ups, and a note-taking structure. Plan appropriate consent before actual sessions.
- **Return:** a research brief, recruitment criteria, and an interview guide tailored to the product. This resource does not recruit participants or collect responses for you. Qualitative interviews do not estimate population prevalence.
- **Fallback:** create the guide locally from the user's research question and existing methodology, marking evidence gaps.

## Product Talk — Opportunity Solution Trees

- **Open:** [Teresa Torres's practical OST guide](https://www.producttalk.org/2016/08/opportunity-solution-tree/).
- **Free scope:** public article and examples. Courses, memberships, and templates hosted in account-based whiteboards are not part of this recommendation.
- **Professional use:** [SuperAwesome's documented rollout](https://www.producttalk.org/adopting-opportunity-solution-trees/) describes product managers and teams applying and adapting trees.
- **Use:** connect one desired outcome to observed customer opportunities, candidate solutions, and assumption tests. Attach interview or observation IDs to opportunity nodes. Mark untested branches explicitly.
- **Return:** a compact evidence-linked tree in Markdown or a local drawing. Invented opportunities and AI-generated personas cannot substitute for discovery inputs; without evidence, provide a hypothesis map and an interview plan.
- **Fallback:** a text outline with the same relationships. No whiteboard account is necessary.

## Excalidraw — browser whiteboard and low-fidelity sketches

- **Open:** [Free editor](https://excalidraw.com/); [official project and supported features](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw).
- **Free scope:** basic drawing in the browser and local file workflows. Excalidraw+ accounts, hosted workspaces, and premium features are excluded. Save a local copy; browser storage is not a durable repository.
- **Professional use:** [a product manager's firsthand workflow](https://onlyrss.org/posts/drawing-with-excalidraw.html) explains using it for app structure and concept sketches.
- **Use:** draw the current workflow, a user journey, an opportunity tree, or one screen for a concept test. Keep observations, hypotheses, and proposals visually distinct. Prefer local export over publishing a sharing link.
- **Return:** an editable drawing and, when useful, an image. A concept sketch tests understanding only when shown to suitable users; creating it is not validation.
- **Fallback:** a local Markdown diagram or simple table. Label locally generated artifacts accurately rather than claiming the website created them.

## Nielsen Norman Group — usability inspection

- **Open:** [Ten usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).
- **Free scope:** public article and examples; paid reports and training are excluded.
- **Professional grounding:** the method's author publishes the research basis and practical examples. This establishes professional methodology, not a named PM's adoption of a particular app.
- **Use:** walk through a specific task on a concept or existing product; record the observed issue, affected step, relevant principle, and plausible consequence. Attribute the method rather than copying the article into the deliverable.
- **Return:** a prioritized issue list and usability-test questions. Inspection finds hypotheses about friction; it neither replaces user testing nor proves conversion impact.
- **Fallback:** inspect the workflow against explicit task success, clarity, recovery, and control criteria, labeling the exercise an internal review.

## Google PAIR — AI product design methods

- **Open:** [People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/) and [practical patterns](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/patterns).
- **Free scope:** public chapters, examples, and workshop material. No model API, cloud project, or paid AI service is required to read and apply the guidance.
- **Professional grounding:** Google PAIR describes input from industry practitioners and links product case studies. Use it as an established design resource, not a current model benchmark or a popularity claim.
- **Use:** choose patterns relevant to the task: AI suitability, user expectations, control, error handling, or data evaluation. Define a non-AI baseline and an appropriate human fallback.
- **Return:** AI acceptance criteria, failure scenarios, and evaluation questions. The guide does not demonstrate the quality of a specific model or establish its current price.
- **Fallback:** document baseline, error costs, review needs, and quality tests from first principles. Verify changing model capabilities and prices separately against official sources.
